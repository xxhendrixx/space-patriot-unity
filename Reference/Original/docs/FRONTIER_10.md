# Frontier 10: physical controls, shipboard play and living markets

The standard start is the assigned home hangar (Earth on a new profile). Press L to raise the 26-metre launch lift. Ship → Stations & home changes the home world or returns to the pad. Physical cockpit switches use calibrated image-space click areas. Their labels are tooltips; there is no floating switch panel. Distinct bezel controls open navigation, radar, weapons, systems, crew and stations. Tab and Esc control the generated terminal.

The new terminal image is `assets/ui/pause-terminal-v10.png`; the exact generation prompt is `docs/pause-terminal-prompt.txt`. Live content, buttons and tab labels are rendered above it. Rifle and sidearm artwork share a transform with the muzzle flash, and their muzzle is centered horizontally. Weapon-specific recoil changes aim and camera, recovers with a critically damped spring, and does not rotate a ship's flight heading. Ballistic shot origins are projected from the illustrated muzzle toward the center aim point.

First-person movement accepts gameplay keys after hardware-button clicks, restores canvas focus on menu dismissal, and places a standing crew member beside the chair. The deck runs in ship-local coordinates while the vehicle keeps moving. Friends board within 150 m, retain their own ship, and occupy host-validated engineering and dorsal laser stations. The host computes the turret muzzle from the owner's vessel. Station requests publish the latest player position and receive an explicit host acknowledgement; older snapshots cannot re-seat a player during a pending stand request. Guest boarding is a proximity transition, not a continuously traversable exterior ramp. Airlocks require landing/docking.

The home hangar has opening roof panels, a launch platform, and walls in the movement field. Cities place 16 buildings and at least 48 floors in a compact district. ArchitectureWorks geometry, movement volumes and shot colliders remain connected. Z opens the nearest machine computer or lift controls. MachineWorks reactor commands affect powered machinery and lifts. City geometry uses a dither fade between detailed rooms and distant buildings. Terrain blends over the approach range rather than replacing the planetary representation in one frame.

Markets simulate production, consumption, scarcity, purchases, sales, delivery contracts, ship ownership, repair/rearm charges, autonomous convoys and faction treasuries. Sealed contract cargo cannot be sold, and contracts persist with inventory. Remote markets update without rendering their cities; nearby convoy ships are shown. Markets are host-owned in a crew session. The simulation runs while the session is active, not while the application is closed. Faction tactical combat remains concentrated in the active city district; this does not claim a full persistent MMO.

Planet orbits solve Kepler's equation with catalog periods. Solar rotation and obliquity use the [NASA Planetary Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/). Exoplanet synchronous rotation is an assumption, not an observation. Game distances are compressed and epoch phases are fictional. The rotational transforms carry terrain sampling, settlements, actors and landed ships. The 1× / 60× / 3600× clock is controlled by the host. Weatherworks supplies local atmospheric precipitation and Fireworks supplies real ship-nozzle particles.

Rendering resize/adaptive-resolution changes now happen before drawing. Resizing the primary canvas after drawing cleared its buffer and could expose a black frame. The regression checks the animation-loop resize/draw sequence, GPU error state, keyboard movement, recoil, home launch, sealed deliveries, salvage completion and distinct screen buttons. Multiplayer checks cover two browser peers on one computer; cross-NAT and eight-player load remain unverified.

Z is context-sensitive: it operates on-foot doors, seats, elevators, machines and computers, and frees/recaptures the HUD cursor while piloting. Q/E roll, Ctrl descends, R reloads and G cycles landing gear. Shift provides a 1.35× local speed limit, tactical burst is capped at 1.6×, and the separate CRUISE switch engages faster travel. Decoupled thrust preserves sideways momentum; assisted flight and braking slow it. H acquires a visible world, station or beacon in the flight reticle, including worlds in the same system. Planet occlusion prevents targeting through a nearer planet. Arrival retains route and terrain safety interlocks.

City computer and lift screens use emissive canvas textures with actual MachineWorks power/integrity/command values and elevator floor/target/phase. They refresh at up to five updates per simulation second and share the simulation used by physical interactions.

Settings identifies the WebGL graphics device independently of the optional timer-query readout. The Chrome verification environment reports ANGLE Metal on Apple M2 Pro; this does not establish the renderer used by a separate live in-app browser. GPU world-pass timing covers the base shader pass, not all scene rendering or whole-device utilization.
